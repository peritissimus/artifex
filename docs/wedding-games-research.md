# Wedding newspaper games: research and revision

Reviewed 12 September 2026. Format: two single-sided A2 landscape sheets, nested into four printed A3 pages. These games must work at home when the save-the-date arrives, with pencil and paper.

## References inspected

- [Elation Event newspaper on Pinterest](https://in.pinterest.com/pin/281543726115310/): inspected in Chrome. Varied puzzle shapes and compact editorial sections gave the spread more rhythm than a large crossword followed by lists.
- [Newspaper Club: real wedding newspapers](https://www.newspaperclub.com/blog/2021/05/21/6-ways-to-wow-your-wedding-guests-with-a-newspaper): Joel Avery's personal crossword and Maya and Cody's large maze are useful examples of building entertainment into a newspaper.
- [Fiona Lau Events: wedding games](https://fionalauevents.ca/weddinggames/): the fill-in-the-blank story format invites guests to create the joke. Its Name That Tune activity needs music and a host, so it is less suitable for a mailed save-the-date.
- [Crossword Your Event: wedding puzzles](https://crossword-your-event.com/en/wedding/): personal memories and shared experiences are better clue material than generic wedding vocabulary.

These are format references. No template artwork, puzzle boards, or story text was copied. The grouping board, travel story, maze and wording are original to this implementation.

## What changed

1. **Personal crossword:** retained the requested game, reduced its oversized footprint, put the clues alongside it and rewrote them with a lighter voice. Names are accessible warm-ups; Malibu Town and the monthly relationship duration add variety. More personal anecdotes would improve the content further.
2. **Perfect matches:** replaced the easy place scramble with sixteen words that form four compound-word groups. Guests can solve alone or together. Each tile has a space for a group number; four lines collect the connecting words. Answers are on page 4.
3. **One more detour:** replaced the generic voting list with a real maze. Start in Gurugram, pass the Rishikesh checkpoint, finish in Agra. The maze is a connected tree with one solution; the checkpoint lies on that route. A small solution appears on page 4. It is a playful diagram, not a geographical map.
4. **Stop the press:** guests supply six words before seeing an original fictional travel report. This produces a shared punchline without asserting invented couple anecdotes as fact. The report is explicitly labelled unverified fiction in tone and context.
5. **Layout:** smaller inside mastheads, varied puzzle shapes, numbered game headings, writing lines and short newspaper-style jokes. Cover contents and back-page solutions updated to match.

## Decisions

- Omit reception bingo, guest scavenger hunts and hosted trivia: these need the wedding crowd or an MC, while the invitation should be playable upon receipt.
- Avoid unsupported “who said it” or “two truths and a lie”: we do not yet have verified funny incidents or quotes from the couple.
- Keep the monochrome newspaper typography and both single-sided sheets. No phone, QR code or purchase is needed to play.
